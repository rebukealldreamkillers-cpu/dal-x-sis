There is a gap in how organizations deploy AI systems. It is not a gap in capability — AI systems are increasingly capable of generating code, decisions, recommendations, and autonomous actions at scale. The gap is in governance: who or what has the authority to validate AI-generated work before it executes?

This is the problem that Decision Governance addresses.

## What Decision Governance Is

Decision Governance is the discipline of establishing, maintaining, and enforcing accountability for decisions — specifically decisions made by or with AI systems — before those decisions produce effects in the world.

It is not an audit trail. It is not a compliance checkbox. It is a structural layer that sits between what an AI system proposes and what an organization accepts as authorized action.

Decision Governance asks: **Who authorized this? Within what scope? Under what conditions?**

These questions are not new. Organizations have always needed to answer them for human decisions. What is new is that AI systems are forcing these questions to be answered at machine speed, at machine scale, and increasingly without human review in the loop.

## The Execution Gap

When a human makes a significant decision, implicit governance structures engage: peer review, approval hierarchies, legal counsel, institutional memory, organizational authority. These checks are not always efficient, but they exist because organizations learned — often at significant cost — that unchecked decisions create disproportionate risk.

AI systems, as currently deployed, largely bypass these structures. An AI generates an output. That output is executed. The speed is the feature. The absence of a governing layer is the problem.

We call this the **Execution Gap**: the absence of an active governing structure between AI output and real-world effect.

The Execution Gap is not a failure of AI capability. It is a failure of organizational design — a structural oversight in how AI systems have been integrated into decision-making chains.

## Runtime Authority

Runtime authority is the concept we use to describe what fills the Execution Gap.

A runtime authority is not a pre-execution policy document written months in advance, nor a post-execution audit conducted after damage is done. It is an **active layer** — present at the moment of execution — that validates whether an AI-generated work product has the standing to proceed.

Runtime authority answers three questions at the point of execution:

1. **Scope** — Was this decision made within its authorized domain?
2. **Conformance** — Does this decision conform to the governance framework currently in effect?
3. **Acknowledgment** — Has the appropriate authority structure acknowledged and accepted accountability for this decision?

Without runtime authority, AI systems operate in a governance vacuum. They are fast, capable, and unaccountable — not by design, but by absence of design.
